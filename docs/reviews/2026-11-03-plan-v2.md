# Plan v2 — vendor-benchmarked, prior-art first (2026-11-03)

*Replaces the milestone set in `2026-11-02-cnc-milestone-1-review.md` §4 where the two disagree. Written
after the Makerables calibration (#955) showed that Makera Studio cuts a hardwood tool tray in 48 minutes
at 99.5 % coverage while Case Maker refuses the same part as filed and, when coaxed, takes 23 hours. The
first plan should have caught this; it did not, because it judged the engine against its own oracle and
never put a vendor program beside ours. This plan fixes the method of judging first, then the methods
of cutting, in the order that gets a clean part off the machine soonest.*

---

## 0. What the first plan missed, and the rule that replaces it

Three misses, all mine:

1. **No external yardstick.** The gate's ten statements (§2.3(e) of the review) all compare the program
   with the design or with the CAM's own plan. None compares it with what a competent CAM does for the
   same part. "Efficiency measures" were specified as numbers to print, not as a line to beat.
2. **The 3D engine's method was never questioned.** The review listed its budgets, caps and coverage
   shortfall as defects to fix and accepted decision 2 ("in-house heightmap/dexel engine") as settled.
   The method — sample the goal into a 0.1 mm heightfield, plan move by move inside a memory budget —
   is not how any shipping CAM works, and no amount of tuning makes a 110 × 140 mm part fit it.
3. **No prior-art step.** Nothing in the marching orders asked a design note to say what the standard
   method is, where it is implemented, and under what licence, before code was written. Every 3D
   package of the last two weeks (#846, #869, #881, #889, #902, #909, #914, #916, #945, #946) tuned the
   same engine.

**The rule from here:** no engine design note is approved without (a) the standard method named with its
sources, (b) the vendor's own output for a reference part measured on our checker, and (c) a numeric
line against that vendor output. No bench cut, no "Matches", no closure on the path without the
reference beside it.

## 1. The evidence: how Makera Studio actually cuts, and how we do

Scored with the same multi-tool checker on the app's own goal heightfield (`.scratch/makerables/README.md`
on dave-1; the issue is #955):

| part (hardwood) | who | cutters | cycle | top-surface coverage | cut-into > 0.1 mm | rapids / hops |
|---|---|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Tool tray 110 × 140 × 27 | Makera Studio | ⌀6 flat, ⌀3.175 flat, ⌀6 ball, 90° chamfer | **47.6 min** | 99.5 % | 3.1 % (0.4 % without the chamfer — edge breaks the STEP lacks) | ~384 |
| same part | Case Maker, as filed (vise, 0.1 mm) | — | **refused** (`goal-program-too-big`) | — | — | — |
| same part | Case Maker, vise, 0.2 mm | — | plans, then **refuses itself** (finish into both jaws) | — | — | — |
| same part | Case Maker, tape, 0.2 mm | ⌀2 ball, ⌀0.5 ball | **23 h 10 min** (43 % air) | 98.6 % | 0 % | **7,889** |
| Tool organizer 170 × 170 × 27 | Makera Studio | ⌀6 flat, ⌀3.175 flat, ⌀2 flat, ⌀6 ball, chamfer | 47.8 min | 99.0 % | 2.3 % (1.6 % without the chamfer) | ~1,300 |

How Studio does it, read from its NC: **feature pockets and contours at Z levels derived from the model's
planar faces** (the ⌀6 flat first, the ⌀3.175 where it cannot reach), the outer contour in 1 mm steps
to full depth, a **surface-following raster with a FLAT cutter where the surface is gentle** (the finger
scoop: 10,667 feed lines, 14 rapids), the ball only where the surface is curved, chamfer passes on the
rims, and stay-down linking throughout. Its engraving on the mandala coasters runs at one constant Z,
the same constant-depth V-carve model Case Maker chose (#221) — so that decision stands.

Two consequences. First, **most of what the market calls "3D" on a desktop mill is 2.5D from a model**:
planar faces become pockets, the curved remainder is a short surfacing pass. Our engine treats every
model as a surfacing problem, which is why it is slow where Studio is fast. Second, the published model
is not always what the maker cut (the tray's underside recess is not in Makera's program), so the
reference for coverage is the vendor's NC, scored per side, not the STEP.

## 3. What "success" means now (the lines, all numeric)

For every reference part on the demo page (#955), with Makera's program scored on the same checker:

| line | pass |
|---|---|
| coverage (top-surface per side; whole-surface after setups for figures, per the maintainer's ruling) | ≥ vendor − 1 point, and ≥ 95 % |
| cut-into | 0 beyond the edge-break allowance (cells within the chamfer bit's reach of a design edge, no deeper than its leg) |
| cycle time (the app's own estimate, #927) | ≤ 2× the vendor's; goal 1.25× |
| air fraction | ≤ vendor's + 10 points |
| rapids per 1,000 feed lines | ≤ 2× vendor's |
| cutters | ≤ vendor's count + 1, and only cutters the location owns for a bench card |
| posts as filed | yes, in the vendor's own holding, without an operator changing the pitch |

For the bench samples (M1): the accepted §4.4 acceptance numbers, unchanged, plus predicted time within
10 % of the clock.

### M2 — Model-driven 2.5D: cut a STEP or mesh the way Studio does (the biggest lever)

The tray and the organizer are the acceptance parts. From a STEP (we already read B-reps through
OpenCascade, #774) and, second, from a mesh:
1. **Planar-face recognition**: group faces by normal and height; each horizontal face at depth d
   bounded by walls is a pocket floor or an island top; the outer silhouette is the contour; non-planar
   faces are the curved remainder.
2. **The 2.5D ops we already have, driven by those features**: Z-level pocketing per feature with the
   largest flat that fits (rest with the next flat), the outer contour in step-downs to full depth with
   tabs where the part frees, chamfer passes on rims the model carries.
3. **Curved remainder**: a surface-following raster with a flat where the slope is under the flat's
   admissible angle, a ball where it is not — the M3 engine's finishing op, used small.
4. **Cost-priced cutter sequence** and **stay-down linking** (shared with M3).
5. Lines: the tray and organizer at the §3 numbers against Makera's NC. Expected: within 1.5× of 48 min.

### M3 — True 3D surfacing on prior art (#956; the figures)

The design note first (B, 2 days): cutter-location surface by drop-cutter on the triangles for flat,
ball, bull and V cutters; Z-level roughing of stock minus the CL surface with the largest flat; rest by
CL-surface difference with the next cutter; ball finishing at constant scallop (parallel/scallop by
slope); pencil for creases; linking; memory proportional to the mesh; no budgets. References: OpenCamLib
(LGPL-2.1, has emscripten and Node builds in-tree — usable as a WASM library under LGPL, or as the
algorithm reference for a clean-room port), Kiri:Moto (MIT, JavaScript, may be ported with attribution),
FreeCAD Path's 3D Surface / Waterline ops (which call OpenCamLib). Lines: the elephant and panther at
stick scale pass the maintainer's whole-surface line after the setups the app proposes; the wolf and
photo reliefs within 2× of a vendor relief (wild-spirit-3d-relief, CC0, on the page).

### Cross-cutting — the demo page is the gate for all three

#955's card form (vendor program beside ours, §3 lines printed) becomes the only "Matches" the page
awards for a model-driven part. The 12-hour report carries the vendor-delta table.

## 5. Process additions (on top of the marching orders)

1. **Prior art first.** Every engine design note names the standard method, two implementations with
   licences, and the vendor's measured output for a reference part, before code. The manager refuses a
   note without them.
2. **Vendor reference before judgment.** A card with no vendor program beside it is "unreferenced",
   never "Matches". Session E keeps the reference set growing (two per operation class).
3. **No budgets as fixes.** A refusal on memory or time is a defect in the method, filed as such; caps
   are not re-priced.
4. **One engine per method behind one interface.** M2 and M3 land as packages with a public interface
   (`cam/model2d/`, `cam/surface3d/`); `generateGoalProgram` calls them; nothing new goes into
   `generateOneProgram` (#929's intent, now mandatory).
5. **Stop rule.** A package whose cycle estimate on its reference is more than 3× the vendor's is not
   "done" whatever its coverage reads.
6. **The figures stay on the page** at their current numbers until M3 moves them; no further tuning of
   the old engine (held branches named on #943).

## 7. Decisions (the maintainer, 2026-11-03, one by one)

1. **Bench gate:** *"We are waiting for the rebuilt engine, no point doing any other development until
   that engine is in."* So: no bench session of any kind, no stick samples, no Phase 2 landings, no
   wave-3 items until the engine passes the references. #943 line 1b becomes "the rebuilt engine passes
   the seven lines of §3 on the reference cards, then the figures on the whole-surface line".
2. **The engine is ONE rebuild: M2 + M3 together** — model-driven 2.5D and true surfacing as one package
   behind one interface (`cam/model/`), cutter sequence and linking shared. (#956 re-scoped.)
3. **More sessions on the rebuild**, split by module after B's design note: B the CL surface, rough/rest,
   finish, the figures' setups and integration (#957, #959, #960, #965, #966); A planar-face recognition
   and the edge-break rule (#958, V2-2); C the cutter-sequence cost model and linking (#961, #962); D the
   job model and UI seams (#963); F the specs and parity harness, starting now (#964); E the reference
   cards, now (#955). The manager reviews every hunk; A and the manager review the note.
4. **Licence:** port from Kiri:Moto (MIT) with attribution; OpenCamLib (LGPL-2.1) is the algorithm
   reference only — re-implemented from its descriptions, no LGPL code copied or linked.
5. **The sticks are the samples.** No 110 × 140 blank; the tray and organizer stay simulation-only
   reference cards; the bench resumes with the five stick samples.

**What this does to §4 and §6:** M1's bench half (S1–S6, #936–#941, #932–#935) is parked until the rebuild
passes; M1's measurement half (the gate, #924/#948/#925, the edge-break rule, #955) continues because it
judges the engine; M2 and M3 merge into the one rebuild (#956 + #957–#966); V2-3/V2-4 (cost pick, linking)
are #961/#962 inside it; V2-6…V2-15 are #958, #959/#960, #957, #965, #966.

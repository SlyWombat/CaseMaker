Forms, one sentence each

  • Plate flat, cut from the top.
  • Block as tall as it is wide; sides may be cut in later setups.
  • Sheet thin; in the vise on a waste board, or taped to the bed.
  • Printed from a printer; layers known.
  • Disc / Rod round; vise or tape for a face job, the rotary when cut around.

The word decides nothing

Today plate, block and sheet are the same thing in the engine: the same three fields, the same mount, the same facing rule. The form stays as the picture that sets the size fields and tells a new operator what the words mean; step 3 works out how the numbers sit in what is on the machine.

Rules

  • Home before hands go in the machine; the work light on while they are there; home when done. These are the bench rules today, as memory and bench tools; here they become the product's.
  • The outcome is a change of the active bed profile and a history entry. The job that asked is re-checked against it; a frozen job is re-frozen if its mount changed.
  • The directed cutter change inside a run is this same wizard: steps 2, the change, 5.

Replaces

  • Nothing: today there is no way to tell the product the vise has come off, and the only guided physical steps are the run's bit change and the registration touches.

Remove · move · keep

Remove

  • The second modal and its numbering
  • The full-page Cut-this-blank wizard and its inline Machines card
  • “Use the badge blank” headline; the template cards in the stock step
  • The disabled-rotary sentence; New text
  • Checked / Not checked paragraphs under Generate
  • Issue numbers and codes in text
  • The project Save/Load/New/Export on CNC pages

Move

  • The fourteen-type picker → step 4 and “Add operation”, grouped
  • The machine check → the strip
  • The oracle numbers → Simulate
  • The clearance acknowledgement → Cut step 1
  • “X runs between the jaws” → the Holding picture
  • Provenance → one sentence in the Technical fold

Keep

  • The gate and its four ticks
  • The registration plan and its words
  • The run sheet
  • Every refusal's sentence (in its new place)
  • The matrix's fourteen operation names
  • Every testid the panel redesign kept
  • The engine: nothing here changes a toolpath

Acceptance, when built

  1. A new operator on this house, with no machine on the network, reaches a generated, verified, simulated engraving of their own text from the door in under two minutes and eight clicks, changing no default, and never sees a disabled control without its reason.
  2. Every screen has exactly one step counter: “Step n of 4” in the wizard, “1–4” in Cut.
  3. The walk-through transcripts contain no #\d, no -default or -refused tokens, no vendor label as a cutter name, no field without a unit, no decimal beyond three places.
  4. The built flow's captures match these mockups screen for screen.

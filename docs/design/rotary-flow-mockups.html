Rotary Job Flow

The first-job flow, extended to a job held in the rotary chuck. The same four steps and the same four Cut lines; what makes a job rotary is the stock (round, or squared) and the mount (the chuck). Each mockup carries the exact copy of docs/design/rotary-flow.md; the notes beside it say where every default comes from.

Nothing new counts steps. A rotary job is a first-job job; the rotary is on the pins or the change wizard puts it there. Until the module is measured on this machine, the Run line carries one sentence and the rest of the flow works.

Principles added for the rotary

First-job principles 1–8 hold unchanged. These six are added.

  1. A rotary job is a first-job job. No project type, no second wizard. The form and the mount decide.
  2. The rotary is on the pins, or the job is taken there. The machine's state is read; the chuck is offered with its cost and the change wizard.
  3. The operator thinks on the surface. Items are placed in mm along and around; degrees only on angle fields and the A read-out, with ° every time.
  4. Two pictures, one geometry. The map for placing, the turning stock for watching; both draw the same opened regions.
  5. The run path waits for the bench, and says so once, at the Run line: The rotary module has not been measured on this machine yet — this program can be checked and saved; running it waits for the module's bench check.
  6. What the cutter cannot do on a round stock is refused by name, at the item: through the centre, off the line over the axis, into the jaws or the tailstock.

Which form goes where

  • Rod: the vise on its end up to 20 mm (the vise's floor depth); the chuck when it fits ⌀80 × 150.
  • Tube: the chuck; the wall caps every depth.
  • Block: squared stock in the chuck when its diagonal fits (Makera CAM's Square stock).
  • Disc: on its face, as today; not offered in the chuck.
  • Plate, Sheet, Printed: flat; not offered.

Sources

⌀80 × 150: Makera's database row for the Z1. ⌀20 × 100: the recommended first rotary stock. Rod rule: the existing rod sentence, which no longer blocks Next once Holding writes the chuck.

Defaults and where they come from

  • Jaws reverse — Makera's guide: reverse jaws fitted by default for larger work; standard for small parts. The jaw range is not published; until the bench calipers it, ⌀80 × 150 is the only gate.
  • Grip 15 mm — Case Maker's default, a shop setting. Makera publishes no jaw depth.
  • Stick-out — derived: length − grip. Shown, not asked.
  • Tailstock in — Makera's shipped value (Studio's rotary relief, haveTailTop: true); the centre hole is Makera's guide.
  • Chuck on the left, X 0 at the jaws — Makera's preset zero "at the centre of the right edge", read as the chuck's face.

Replaces

  • The chuck option that reads the job is held in the vise and writes nothing. Selecting it now stores the chuck.

The groups are the classes

  • On the surface (wrap): text and shapes on the map, cut below the surface; A and X move together on every stroke.
  • On a face (indexed): turn to an angle, hold, cut flat work. A Flat's + Add operation offers the flat picker at that angle.
  • 3D (relief): Makera's Rotation Relief. Authored, generated, simulated and saved; the machine does not run it. simulate only is a fact tag, like recommended.

Not an operation

Cut-off. Makera offers none, and a vertical end mill can only part a rod off by cutting through the centre. The part is sawn off; a Pocket band all the way round marks the line.

V-carve

Shown when the rotary simulation sweeps a V-bit (today it sweeps a flat end only). Until then the picker hides it.

Defaults

Reads along, like a pencil's lettering. Faces all round: 6, the largest across-flats inside the ⌀ (⌀ · cos(180°/n)).

Rules

  • Items are placed and dimensioned in mm, along and around (author on the surface). A selected item also reads its centre angle in °.
  • The seam is where the map's edges meet on the stock, at 0° by default: where A homes. An item may cross it; A is never wrapped, so the cut is continuous, and the map repeats a strip each side so it is drawn whole.
  • The bands: grip + 3 mm keep-out at the jaws, 10 mm at the tailstock while it is in. Case Maker's defaults with their provenance on hover, until measured.
  • Map | Stock shows the same opened regions on the cylinder with the A-zero mark. Both use the wider opening around (r · R / (R − d)) so a stroke that merges on one merges on the other.
  • A depth that reaches the centre, or past a tube's wall, is refused at the depth field.

At the surface

The program's F stays the catalogue's mm/min; the controller turns it into a speed of A. The line says what the material sees, at the item's floor radius: rpm = F / (2π·r + 30), at most 10 rpm; surface = rpm × 2π·r. Makera's firmware feed model; the 10 rpm limit read from this machine's config. When the limit binds: the rod turns at its 10 rpm limit; the cut sees 597 mm/min of 1 200.

Rules

  • Stock (default): the stock, the ghost, the gouges and the path turn by A in one group; the cutter, chuck and tailstock stay still. The camera does not chase the turn: the rod's bounding box is the same at every angle.
  • Unroll re-meshes the same columns onto the map, with what was cut.
  • A reads in ° within the turn plus the turns, never the unwound total alone. Z reads as above the axis.
  • The oracle checks every column of the map: the floor radius inside each letter's opened region, the surface outside, one grid cell each way plus the facet a flat cutter leaves on a curve (√(Z² + r²) − Z). The numbers live behind Technical.
  • Indexed jobs mark each face's index as a tick on the scrubber and show the stock turned to it.

The touch plan

  • Both sides at the axis height: their middle is the axis's Y, their spread the diameter (the two-touch centre find Stepcraft and Tormach use).
  • The top at four turns: the mean less the radius is the axis's Z; the spread is how far the rod sits off the axis, and out of round.
  • The free end: X, so the map's along matches the rod.
  • A: where it homes, the seam. An indexed job aligned to a feature adds one touch on it.

The gate

Registration is allowed before the bench check: it moves the probe, not a cutter, and its readings are the bench check's own. Run, upload and release refuse with the one sentence. The probed radius replaces the nominal; the run sheet prints both.

Simulate before the bench

A fixture job per class, each with an oracle that needs no bench number; Makera's own programs replay as references.

On the surface

  • Name on a dowel — every default; golden numbers: arc 15.708 mm = A 90°; opening 0.526 mm around
  • Band across the seam — one continuous cut
  • Pocket band all the way round — floor at r 9 on every column
  • Into the jaws — the refusal at the row

On a face

  • Two flats at 0° and 180°, the chord volume per face
  • Hex all round, across flats 17.321
  • Cross-hole ⌀3 through at 90°

3D · simulate only

  • Shallow relief band — stays ≥ 3 mm above the axis; simulates
  • Through the centre — refused at the first move past the axis
  • Makera's relief rough simulates in full; its finish is refused at move 3 847; the air test is the rough with another tool number

Bench-only: which way +A turns · steps per degree on the device · the real A speed and the feed scaling · whether Home homes A · the axis height and the chuck face · the preset's repeatability · the jaw range and tailstock reach · the round-stock file header · the unwind on the device · run-out and backlash · feeds at radius.

Acceptance, when built

  1. From the door, with no machine on the network, an operator reaches a generated, verified, simulated, oracle-passing wrap engraving of their own text on a dowel, changing no default, and never sees a disabled control without its reason. One e2e spec.
  2. The same job in Cut, with the module unmeasured: lines 1–2 tick, line 3 offers registration, line 4 reads the bench sentence verbatim and has no button; upload and release refuse with the same sentence.
  3. Selecting “In the rotary chuck” stores the chuck with jaws, grip, stick-out and tailstock.
  4. The change wizard fits the rotary as Makera's guide says, and writes “Rotary on the pins · wasteboard off”.
  5. Every class simulates its fixture in the suite; Makera's two relief programs pin their outcome when present.
  6. The transcripts contain no issue number, no rotary code, no A without °, no angle field without °, no length without mm, no decimal past three; the bench is named once, at the Run line.
  7. The built screens match these mockups, station for station.

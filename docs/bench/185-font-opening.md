# #185 — font-opening report: what each cutter actually reaches

Generated by `scripts/font-opening-report.ts` (re-run it to regenerate). Every number is
#171's morphological opening — `offset(offset(glyph, −r), +r)` — measured by the app's own
`measureLabels`, at the same radii the Engrave panel warns about. **Nothing here was cut.**

**The question.** `/Fabrication.md` §7.4 said "a 4 mm cap-height title has a stroke around
0.5 mm" on the strength of a guess. This replaces the guess with a measurement: which strokes
survive the 1.0 mm flat end mill, and which the V-bit reaches that the flat end does not.

## What was measured, and the one approximation

- **Faces:** all six bundled faces — Barlow (sans), IBM Plex Mono (mono), IBM Plex Serif (serif), regular and bold.
- **Words:** `CASE` and `MAKER` — the app's own default-job labels, so the
  sample is not invented and any ratio here can be reproduced by typing the same word.
- **Cap heights:** 4 mm, 6 mm, 8 mm, 10 mm, 14 mm.
- **Cutters:** the ⌀1.0 mm flat end (r = 0.5), and a 30° included V-bit for tip ⌀ ∈ {0.1, 0.5} mm
  over #165's ladder depths 0.6 / 0.8 / 1 / 1.2 / 1.4 / 1.6 mm.

**Ratios are per-word.** `ratio` = the opened glyph area ÷ the ideal glyph area, so a word whose
letters are mostly thick verticals scores higher than one with thin diagonals at the same cap
height — which is why the tables name the word. The *tolerance* in §5 is a different animal: it
is set by the face's thinnest stroke and barely moves between these two words at all.

**The approximation:** a V-groove is a wedge, not a cylinder. `w(d) = tip + 2·d·tan(15°)` is the
groove's surface width at depth `d`, and `w(d)/2` is the radius of the largest circle that fits
inside it. Opening at that radius answers the issue's question — *is this stroke reachable at all* —
but it does not model decision 14's depth coupling: a stroke **wider** than `w(d)` is still reachable,
just at a floor shallower than `d`. So a ✗ here means erased; a ✓ means "cut to full depth".

**The fonts, pinned.** These numbers are these bytes, and nothing else:

| font file                | sha256 (first 12) |
| ------------------------ | ----------------- |
| Barlow-Regular.ttf       | `95aa02c7c430`    |
| Barlow-Bold.ttf          | `84e6a4d61e7c`    |
| IBMPlexMono-Regular.ttf  | `6a3412f058c7`    |
| IBMPlexMono-Bold.ttf     | `ac27abd6450a`    |
| IBMPlexSerif-Regular.ttf | `e882efa9c419`    |
| IBMPlexSerif-Bold.ttf    | `534c02c29599`    |

A font update that moves an outline changes a hash here and the tables with it. The cutter
radius is likewise the only other input: it is `r = w(d)/2` from the formula above, never a
figure read off a tool label (#185 review).

**Precision, measured not assumed.** The opening is exactly scale-invariant, so every cell was
measured twice — once at its own size, once at 2× size with r × 2. The two should be identical and
are not, because opentype flattens glyph curves and Manifold arcs its offsets at absolute mm
tolerances. Over all 780 cells the disagreement is a median of
0.01 pp and a worst case of 2.88 pp — the worst
cases sit exactly on a cliff, where a hundredth of a millimetre decides whether a stroke is cut
at all. **A cell within that worst case of the 90 % line is marked `~`**: it is *at* the line, not
demonstrably on one side of it.

Two things the table says by collapsing what was measured: the tolerated diameter is
**proportional to cap height** — the worst deviation across the five sizes is 0.2 % — so
`⌀ per mm` is the whole row; and `CASE` and `MAKER` cross within a hundredth of a millimetre
of each other, because the crossing is set by the face's thinnest stroke, not by which word you
type.

Across the six faces the tolerated cutter runs from **⌀0.07 to ⌀0.18 per mm of cap
height**. At the 4 mm cap height §7.4's guess was about, that is 6 different answers
spanning ⌀0.27 to ⌀0.70 mm — and the smallest flat end mill in the
catalogue is ⌀1.0 mm, which is 1.4× wider than even the most forgiving face allows.

# 180° indexing and backlash — bench procedure (#757)

**Measurement only: this procedure has NOT been run. No machine was used, and no power was
switched.** It is the recorded bench half of the acceptance test
`casemaker-app/tests/unit/indexingBacklash.spec.ts`; the software half passes on the exact sweep.

## The part

A ⌀30 × 40 mm rod in the rotary chuck (the shop's proxy material, softwood or PLA). Two straight
slots are milled down its length, one at A = 0° and its mirror at A = 180°:

- **Tool:** ⌀6 flat end mill; **spindle** 12000 rpm; **feed** 1200 mm/min, **plunge** 300 mm/min.
- **Each slot:** 12.5 mm deep from the surface, so its floor is 2.5 mm from the rotary axis;
  X from 6 mm (one cutter radius clear of the jaws) to 37 mm (the cutter's edge at the free end).
- **The design's wall:** 5.00 mm through the centre — the material between the two slot floors.
- **Program:** the indexed faces at A 0° then 180°, generated by `cam/indexed.ts` from the fixture
  above: constant A per run, zero lead-in and lead-out, and every turn taken on a rapid at the safe
  height. The app cannot author indexed faces yet (R-UI-11), so the bench file is the fixture's
  posted program, not a job from the Design panel.

The simulation's reference, measured by the unit test: each slot 6.000 mm wide (the cutter's
diameter), the wall 5.000 mm at both sides, square corners, and the removed volume within 1 % of
the closed form of the slot.

## What to measure

1. **The wall, at both sides.** With calipers reading to 0.01 mm, measure the 5 mm wall at the
   +Y side and at the −Y side of the slot, at mid-length. Record both readings, e.g.
   `+Y 5.00 mm`, `−Y 4.85 mm`.
2. **The difference is the rotary's 180° backlash or misalignment** — 0.15 mm in that example. The
   sign follows the side that reads thinner. Record it beside the pair.
3. **The corners.** Inspect both slot corners at the wall and at the floor for rounding or
   overshoot; take a close-up photo and note it.
4. **Feed the figure forward.** Put the difference and the two readings on the rotary
   characterisation (#238).

## Reading the result

The simulation leaves the walls equal by construction, so any bench difference between the two
sides is the machine's index, not the design: the fixture exists to make that difference visible
to a caliper. A difference above 0.05 mm is worth a second part and a note on #238; a difference
near 0.15 mm in the example above is the kind of backlash this test was asked to measure.

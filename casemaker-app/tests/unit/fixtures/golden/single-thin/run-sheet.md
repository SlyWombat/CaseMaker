## 5 · Set the work origin

1. The vise already references X and rotation to within ±0.05 mm, before any touch.
2. Touch 1 of 3: the right edge — the one against the moving jaw, about 19 mm along it from the front end — this gives position and rotation.
3. Touch 2 of 3: the right edge — the one against the moving jaw, about 57 mm along it from the front end — this gives rotation.
4. Touch 3 of 3: the front edge — the one nearest you, at about its middle, 12.7 mm from the left end — this gives Y.
5. Z0: the blank's top face — probe it, or touch off on it. Record the reading AND the tool that made it: on a job cut by two cutters the second program’s Z0 is worked out from this surface and the PRB of whichever tool took this touch, so both go on this one blank — the first cutter’s own PRB is a separate reading, taken where it is fitted. — \_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_  *(surface Z (mm) and the PRB of the tool that set Z0 (mm))*
6. X0 Y0 Z0 is the top-front-left corner of the blank. That leaves the work origin within ±0.02 mm in X and Y and ±0.029° in rotation. Move there and check by eye before going on. How well YOU set it is not modelled: the file places the blank at this corner and judges the jaws against it, so an origin that is out by a little — or a blank sitting differently from what was measured — shifts the whole job relative to both without the app knowing. The only X/Y slack for that is this job's edge margin, 1 mm, which is the gap the content keeps from a stock edge rather than a locating tolerance: a cut standing at that margin has used all of it.
7. Rotation is measured, and a static .nc cannot compensate it (Smoothieware has no variables): apply it in Studio's dialogs, or re-post with the rotation baked into the geometry
8. Z is always probed on the face to be cut: no fixture can supply it
9. The blank's top is measured at 5 points across the cut — its centre, then both extents on each axis — because a printed blank’s colour line is a plane and its dome is extra material above it: one touch at the centre cannot tell you the floor at the ends of a line. Take them in the order below and write each reading down. — \_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_  *(surface Z at each point (mm), and how each was taken (probe / touch-off))*
10. Surface touch 1 of 5: the cut area's centre, X 12.675 Y 38 — take the top there and record the reading.
11. Surface touch 2 of 5: the cut area's left extent, X 5.137 Y 38 — take the top there and record the reading.
12. Surface touch 3 of 5: the cut area's right extent, X 20.213 Y 38 — take the top there and record the reading.
13. Surface touch 4 of 5: the cut area's front extent, X 12.675 Y 1 — take the top there and record the reading.
14. Surface touch 5 of 5: the cut area's back extent, X 12.675 Y 75 — take the top there and record the reading.
15. The floors must stay between 0.4 mm below the top (the colour split, including its 0.2 mm margin) and 0.5 mm below it (the blank's underside, including its 1 mm margin) — a band 0.1 mm wide. A surface spread wider than that band cannot keep every floor past the colour line and clear of the pocket at the same time. — **band 0.1 mm**
16. Set Z0 from the LOWEST of these 5 readings, not from the registration touch above and not from the centre alone. Every programmed floor depth is measured from Z0, so at a higher touch the local floor is deeper by exactly its height above Z0: with Z0 at the lowest reading, the programmed depth is the SHALLOWEST floor and the programmed depth plus the spread is the deepest. — **Z0 = the lowest surface touch** — \_\_\_\_\_\_\_\_  *(the touch Z0 was set from, and the readings it came from)*
